Corrective actions · Practical explainer

When is a finding really closed?

An action marked complete is a claim to verify. Closure should be supported by evidence proportionate to the finding and the agreed action.

Define completion at the start

Agree a specific action, responsible person, realistic deadline and evidence needed. For example, distinguish updating an approval procedure from demonstrating that approvals occur before payment.

Check what changed

Review the new procedure, approval records or other relevant evidence. Where the issue concerns operation of a control, examine subsequent transactions or observe the revised process as appropriate.

Keep the outcome honest

Record whether the action is completed, partially implemented, overdue or awaiting verification. If the issue persists, discuss further action or escalation through the agreed process.

Retain the closure trail

Document the evidence reviewed, the reviewer, date and rationale for closure. A management decision to accept residual risk should be recorded separately from evidence that a control weakness has been fixed.

Try it: Use the findings and action tracker to keep closure evidence visible.
